                About

                Blue Street

                Blue Street turns the raw data behind the currency markets into plain reads, so you can form your own view of where the major currencies are heading and why.

                What it covers

                The eight major currencies (the US dollar, euro, British pound, Japanese yen, Swiss franc, Australian dollar, Canadian dollar and New Zealand dollar), plus gold and oil: what moved them, what is driving them underneath, and what could move them next.

                What it’s for

                A five-minute read. Open it, see what changed, and know where each currency stands. The depth is there for whoever wants it, from each currency’s own page to the full story behind every headline.

                What it believes

                • Meaning before numbers. Every figure comes with what it was, what happened, what it means, and what it does to the currency, in short plain sentences.
                • Evidence, not a verdict. Blue Street weighs the evidence and shows its work. It never tells you to buy or sell; the call is yours.
                • Readable cold. Every page should make sense to someone who hasn’t opened it in a week.

                Who it’s for

                Traders who want a fundamental view behind their charts, and anyone who wants to understand why currencies move without wading through raw data.

                How it’s made

                Blue Street is an independent project, written and updated around the clock by AI assistants working from public data, under a trader’s direction. Every story links to its sources.

                How the scores work

                The strength score

                Each currency gets a score from −7 to +7. It adds up seven factors that drive currencies over weeks and months.

                The seven factors

                • Policy: what the central bank is doing with interest rates, and what markets expect next. Higher rates tend to draw money in.
                • Growth: how strong the economy and the job market look.
                • Inflation: how far prices are running above or below the central bank’s goal, which shapes what it does next.
                • Risk sentiment: whether markets are calm or nervous. Nervous markets favour the yen, the franc and the dollar.
                • Terms of trade: what the country earns for what it sells abroad compared with what it pays for what it buys, like oil for Canada or iron ore for Australia.
                • Positioning: how heavily speculators are betting on the currency. When bets are unusually one-sided, they tend to unwind.
                • Fiscal & sovereign: government budgets, debt and politics, when they shake confidence in the currency.

                How each factor is scored

                Every edition, each factor gets one of three marks for each currency: +1 (supports) when it’s currently a reason for the currency to be stronger, −1 (weighs) when it’s a reason to be weaker, and 0 (neutral) when it isn’t doing much. Positioning follows a fixed rule: a bet in the most extreme tenth of its history counts against the crowd.

                The score adds the seven marks, so it runs from −7 to +7. Every currency page shows exactly how its score adds up under “How it’s scored”.

                Pairs

                A pair’s score is one currency’s score minus the other’s, from the side of your view. The pair picker also shows whether holding the pair pays or costs interest, and the next big event for it.

                What it isn’t

                The score is a summary of the evidence, not a forecast and not a trade signal. Prices can move against the fundamentals for weeks, especially around big news.

                What’s next

                A measured version is being tested: each factor read from data instead of a judgment call, scored on a scale, and checked against years of price history before anything changes here. If it doesn’t do better, it won’t replace this one.

                FAQ

                Questions and answers

                Is this trading advice?
                No. Blue Street is market context only. It explains what’s driving currencies; it never tells you what to trade.
                Why doesn’t it say buy or sell?
                Because the call depends on your timeframe, your risk and your charts. Blue Street gives you the evidence for each side so you can make it.
                When does it update?
                Three editions every weekday, before the London open, the New York open and the New York close, one each weekend morning, and the news through the day. The update schedule has the times.
                Where does the data come from?
                Central banks, official statistics, market prices, the US futures regulator’s positioning reports and major news publishers. See sources.
                What do “Supports” and “Weighs” mean?
                Supports means that factor is currently a reason for the currency to be stronger; weighs means a reason for it to be weaker. How the scores work explains the rest.
                Why is a currency falling when its score is high?
                The score reads the fundamentals, which play out over weeks and months. Day to day, prices also move on surprises, positioning and plain noise.
                Does the score ever change during the day?
                It’s set at each edition. Breaking news shows up on the News tab between editions, and the next edition folds it in.
                What are the arrows on the headlines?
                They show which way a story pushes each currency it touches: up, down, or mixed. It’s the push from that story, not a call on the currency.
                Can I use it like an app?
                Yes. On iPhone, open the site in Safari, tap Share, then “Add to Home Screen”. On Android, use the browser menu’s “Add to Home screen” or “Install app”.
                Does it cost anything?
                No. Blue Street is free while it’s being built.
                Does it track me?
                No. There are no accounts and no analytics. See privacy.
                How do I suggest something or report a mistake?
                A feedback form is coming soon.

                Sources

                Where the data comes from

                Every story and every pillar read links to its own sources. These are the main places Blue Street draws on.

                • Central banks: each bank’s own statements, minutes and rate decisions, with market pricing for upcoming meetings from CME FedWatch and major news reports.
                • Economic data: national statistics agencies, with history from FRED (the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis) and the New York Fed.
                • Market levels: indicative exchange rates, yields, oil and gold from major news services such as Reuters. These are for context, not prices you can trade at.
                • Positioning: the weekly Commitments of Traders report from the CFTC, the US futures regulator.
                • News: major publishers and wire services, credited and linked on every story. Summaries and currency reads are Blue Street’s own.

                Privacy & disclaimer

                The fine print, kept short

                Disclaimer

                Blue Street is market context only. Nothing on it is financial advice or a recommendation to buy or sell anything. Markets are risky; trade only with money you can afford to lose. Prices shown are indicative and can be out of date.

                Privacy

                • No accounts, no analytics, no ads, no tracking.
                • Your settings (light or dark mode, menu style, calendar time zone) are saved only in your own browser.
                • The site’s fonts load from Google Fonts, and story pictures load from the publishers’ own sites, so those services see a normal web request.
                • Links to publishers take you to their sites, which have their own policies.
